Sort out the tabs you already have open

The accounts you are signed into right now are at the top, with the number of your open tabs beside each one, and those are the ones already ticked. Tabs on them move into their session without reloading. Cookies are copied, never moved, so nothing you are in the middle of is disturbed and undoing a mistake costs nothing but deleting a session.

What you have open

The accounts with tabs open come first, then everything else this browser is carrying, ranked by how much each one looks like a real signed-in account rather than a preference cookie. Sites that share one account are grouped together, because a university login covers the portal, the sign-in page and the learning system, and those are one thing rather than three.

READING THE PROFILE JAR

What happens when you press it

  1. Your open tabs get grouped Tabs on the same account go into one session together, named after the account and coloured so they are recognisable at a glance.
  2. Its cookies are copied in The browser's own jar is left exactly as it was.
  3. Open tabs move across Tabs already on those sites join the session. They do not reload, because the cookies are identical.
  4. New tabs follow the site A site exactly one session claims takes new tabs on its own. A site two sessions claim asks you which.

One thing to know first

Setting up copies the cookies you have right now. For most sites that is exactly right. For a big account you also keep using in your normal browser, Google most of all, copying a half-finished login can make the site think your session was stolen and sign you out of it. For those, the safer move is Sign in here on a session, which signs in fresh instead of copying. The guide explains why in one short chapter.

You can skip this

Nothing here is required. Sessions can be made by hand at any time from the toolbar button, and this screen is behind Sessions › Adopt existing whenever you want it back.
